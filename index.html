Yanrui Daisy Ning

Assistant Professor, Department of Environmental Engineering and Earth Sciences (EEES), Clemson University
Director, Subsurface Dynamics Lab

I study how fluids, stress, heat and chemistry change together deep underground, and how to watch those changes as they happen, so that carbon storage, geothermal energy and other uses of the subsurface are safer and more predictable.

The Subsurface Dynamics Lab combines coupled thermal-hydrological-mechanical-reactive (THM-R) modeling, distributed fiber-optic sensing (DFOS) and physics-informed machine learning to understand multiphase flow, fracture growth and fluid–rock interaction in porous and fractured rock.

How we work

Sense the subsurface

Fiber-optic cables in wells and mines record strain, vibration and temperature along their whole length. We turn those records into maps of fractures and deformation.

Model the physics

Coupled simulations predict how fluid pressure, rock stress, heat and chemical reactions affect each other in porous and fractured rock.

Learn from data

Physics-informed machine learning connects measurements and models, speeding up forecasts and finding patterns in large field datasets.

More about our research

Where it matters

  • Carbon storage

    Where to inject CO2, how much a formation can hold, and how to confirm it stays there.

  • Geothermal energy

    Creating and monitoring fracture networks that carry heat from hot rock to the surface.

  • Hydrogen storage

    Storing hydrogen underground and recovering as much of it as possible when it is needed.

  • Critical minerals

    Monitoring rock deformation and seepage to make mining for critical minerals safer.

  • Groundwater

    How water and pressure move through aquifers and fractured rock.

  • Oil and gas

    Fracture design, well spacing and production forecasting for more efficient development.
